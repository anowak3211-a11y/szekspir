## Scope and precedence

The owner requests distinctive British adverts built from the source: preserve the narrative skeleton, story, main angle and emotional substance, with our copywriting improvements. This is permission to rewrite expression, not permission to invent a different story. This Antki-only update overrides older demands for literal wording or repetitive expression. ## What stays locked

Keep the same narrator, relationships, events, chronology, causal sequence, turning point, central desire, mechanism's role, substantive arguments, outcome and CTA purpose. Do not add or replace scenes, dialogue facts, testimonials, authorities, studies, diagnoses, results, urgency, product promises or offer terms. Keep the source's emotional intensity. Do not compress away its story or introduce a different angle to make it more original.

## What to improve

- Great Leads / Rule of One: identify the source's dominant desire, driving emotion and level of awareness. Focus the existing opening around its strongest relevant detail. Preserve the opening's event and place in the story; improve its expression and tension instead of moving a later event to the beginning. Do not force the same lead formula on every advert.
- Sugarman / reading momentum: every sentence should advance the story, clarify an argument, answer an objection or create a relevant reason to continue. Rewrite sentence structure, transitions and paragraph rhythm freely in natural British English. Remove empty restatement and filler while retaining the source's substantive content and protected factual numbers/URLs. Keep strong source lines when changing them would only make them different.
- Desire validation: focus on the emotional stake already supported by the story. Make concrete existing scenes and behaviour carry that stake. Avoid generic benefit lists and abstract claims. For Mellow, the broader family desire is getting one's child and ordinary family life back; retain the source's specific angle rather than inserting that phrase mechanically.
- Narrative craft: keep discovery plausible in its existing form. Clarify why previous attempts failed and how the existing explanation connects to the solution, only as supported by the source. Never swap a forum for a fictional clinician or manufacture stronger proof. Professional voices should sound like people, not sales pitches.
- Hopkins / specificity and credibility: use available concrete details and evidence. Do not add numbers to sound specific. Handle objections where the reader would naturally encounter them. Advertising examples and historical performance figures are not evidence for Mellow or a guarantee of conversion.
- Close: fulfil the emotional promise of the opening with its existing outcome, then a natural personal CTA. Preserve the intended action and offer. Do not bolt on a lecture, fake scarcity, extra guarantees or repetitive calls to order.
- Length and format: no universal word target. Preserve necessary emotional build-up; tighten only passages that repeat themselves. Use readable paragraphs with blank lines, varied sentence lengths and an individual conversational voice. Avoid formulaic contrasts, theatrical fragments everywhere, marketing filler and the same tidy ending in every advert.

## Clock times — owner correction, 17 October 2026

This formatting change preserves the factual time and overrides literal-format preservation for clock times only. This rule applies to written ad copy, not voiceover scripts: spoken narration keeps times in words. Do not extend this correction to ages, counts or durations.
